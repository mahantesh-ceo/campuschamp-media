# BP101T · Lesson 1.1 · Episode 1 of 3: "What is a program?"

**Goal:** A first-semester student with zero coding background understands ONE idea:
a program is a list of clear instructions, in order, that a computer follows exactly.
**Length:** about 5½ minutes at ~115 words per minute, with pauses.
**Style:** Kurzgesagt-style illustrated storytelling for the lesson; Apple-style clean brand moments for the intro and outro.
**Voice:** Anu (cheerful, conversational), speaking directly to the student. No keyword lists; each term is named only after the student has seen the idea.

Lesson 1.1 becomes three episodes:
1. **What is a program?** (this script)
2. **Why Python?** How we talk to computers, and where pharmacy uses Python.
3. **Your first program.** print(), line by line, comments, and your first error.

---

## 0:00–0:15 · Cold open (hook)

**On screen:** Dark scene. A single prescription slides in under a warm desk lamp, then another, then dozens, stacking up. A clock ticks to 9:00 AM.

**Anu:** "Monday morning. A pharmacy in Bagalkot. Two hundred prescriptions waiting. Every one of them is a tiny set of instructions, and the pharmacist follows each one, step by step."

**On screen:** "You already think like a programmer. You just don't know it yet."

## 0:15–0:20 · Brand sting (Apple-style)

**On screen:** Clean white-to-indigo wipe. The Campus Champ mark draws itself. "BP101T · Episode 1 · What is a program?" Two seconds, no narration, a soft chime.

## 0:20–1:05 · A prescription is a set of instructions

**On screen:** One prescription fills the screen, beautifully illustrated. Its three lines light up one by one as Anu reads them:
1. Paracetamol 500 mg
2. Take 1 tablet
3. Three times a day, after food

**Anu:** "Let's look closely at one prescription. Three lines. Paracetamol 500 milligrams. Take one tablet. Three times a day, after food."
"Notice three things. First, every line is a clear instruction. Second, they're written in order. And third, the patient follows them exactly as written."

**On screen:** Three labels pin themselves to the paper: *clear* · *in order* · *followed exactly*.

**Anu:** "Keep these three words in mind. Clear. In order. Followed exactly. Everything in this lesson comes back to them."

## 1:05–1:50 · What if an instruction is not clear?

**On screen:** The second line blurs into "Take tablet". A confused patient silhouette looks at three pill strips: one tablet? two? which one?

**Anu:** "Now imagine the second line just said: take tablet. Which tablet? How many? A person would probably call the doctor and ask."

**On screen:** The lines shuffle so "after food" jumps to the top, out of order.

**Anu:** "And if the lines were jumbled up, the meaning could change completely. With medicines, a small confusion can be dangerous. That's why prescriptions are written clearly, and in order."

## 1:50–2:50 · Meet the computer: very fast, very obedient, zero common sense

**On screen:** A sleek, glowing computer chip assembles from geometric pieces at the centre of the screen. Three tags orbit it: *super fast* · *never tired* · *does exactly what it's told*.

**Anu:** "Now meet the computer. It's incredibly fast. It can do millions of steps in a second, and it never gets tired. But here's the surprising part: it has no common sense at all. It cannot guess what you meant."

**On screen:** A sticky note says "Count the tablets." The chip blinks, confused: "?"

**Anu:** "Tell a computer: count the tablets. It gets stuck. Which tablets? Where? Count them how?"

**On screen:** The note splits into four clear steps that light up in order:
1. Look at the strip
2. Start counting from 0
3. For each tablet, add 1
4. Show the total

The chip happily runs through them. The total "10" pops up.

**Anu:** "But give it clear steps, in order, and it does the job perfectly, every single time."

## 2:50–3:20 · Show, then name: the PROGRAM

**On screen:** The four steps slide together into one glowing card. Big, bold type sweeps across: **PROGRAM**

**Anu:** "A list of clear instructions, written in order, that a computer follows exactly. This has a name. It's called a program."
"So a program is just like a prescription. A prescription is written for a patient. A program is written for a computer."

**On screen:** Side by side: Prescription → patient · Program → computer.

## 3:20–4:00 · Exactly means exactly

**On screen:** A program card says "Show the dose: 10 tablets". Someone meant to type 1. The computer instantly shows "10 tablets" with a red glow.

**Anu:** "Remember, the computer follows instructions exactly. If you write ten by mistake instead of one, it won't stop to ask. It will happily show ten."
"That's why, in programming, just like in pharmacy, small details matter. And don't worry: when a computer gets an instruction it doesn't understand, it tells us. We'll see that in Episode 3."

## 4:00–4:35 · Programs are all around you

**On screen:** A phone, a pharmacy billing counter, a glucometer, a hospital records screen and a lab graph float in, each with a tiny glowing list of steps inside.

**Anu:** "Every app on your phone is a program. The billing software at a medical shop. The glucometer that shows a sugar reading. The hospital system that stores patient records. All of them are lists of instructions that someone wrote, and soon, you'll write them too."

## 4:35–5:05 · Your turn (pause and answer)

**On screen:** "Which one is a program?" with three cards:
A. A computer screen
B. A list of clear instructions a computer follows in order
C. The internet
A 5-second countdown ring, then B glows green.

**Anu:** "Quick check. Which one is a program? Pause the video if you need a moment."
(5 seconds)
"It's B. A list of clear instructions that a computer follows in order. Well done."

## 5:05–5:25 · Recap

**On screen:** Three points build up:
1. A program is a list of instructions for a computer.
2. Instructions must be clear and in order.
3. The computer follows them exactly, with no guessing.

**Anu:** "Let's recap. A program is a list of instructions for a computer. They must be clear, and in order. And the computer follows them exactly."

## 5:25–5:40 · Teaser + CTA (Apple-style outro)

**On screen:** The chip looks at a sentence in English and a sentence in Kannada and shows "?". Then a clean white end card: "Next: Why Python?", a QR code and "Practise free on Campus Champ".

**Anu:** "But here's a problem. A computer doesn't understand English or Kannada. So how do we write instructions it understands? That's the next episode: why Python. See you there!"

## Notes for the Kannada version
- Conversational Kannada as students actually speak it. Technical words stay in English (program, computer, instruction).
- Short sentences: one clip per sentence, so the voice doesn't skip words.
- Local touches: Bagalkot pharmacy, medical shop, glucometer.
